## Threat model

Protect against tenant or service impersonation; stolen service credentials; malicious or compromised workloads; cross-tenant confused-deputy access; replay, reordering and downgrade; payload or price/contract alteration; recipient substitution; stale/revoked grants; webhook forgery; duplicate financial or inventory effects; compromised keys; insider misuse; log tampering; region/residency leakage; and offline clients presenting queued work as confirmed.

## Proposed trust and message flow

1. Use the Eventiva top-level key only as a trust/governance root: certify or authorize subordinate identities and publish versioned trust metadata/revocations. Never use its private key for ordinary request signing or payload decryption.
2. Give each tenant gateway and shared service distinct, independently revocable identities. Separate identity/attestation, signing and encryption key purposes; do not assume one subordinate keypair serves all three.
3. Mutually authenticate endpoints over TLS, potentially mTLS where deployable. Before accepting a request, resolve both service identity and request-scoped participant/recipient grant; transport identity alone grants no marketplace data access.
4. Canonicalize and sign security-relevant request metadata and payload digests, including protocol version, sender, intended recipient/service, tenant and participant scope, action, correlation/idempotency key, timestamp/expiry and nonce. Reject unknown versions, stale windows, replayed nonces and recipient/scope mismatch.
5. For fields requiring protection beyond transport termination, use application-layer envelope encryption to the intended recipient/service. Routine payload decryption uses service/workload encryption keys or short-lived data keys, never the Eventiva root private key.
6. Bind accepted messages to A1: authorization and approvals are revalidated as required; successful authoritative mutations atomically persist domain change, idempotency evidence, state/history, outbox event and receipt/audit. Cross-tenant work remains a recoverable saga/outbox workflow, not false global ACID.
7. Provider callbacks require provider-supported signature verification, replay controls and idempotent reconciliation to supplier and buyer ledgers. Preserve Eventiva's non-custodial connected-account payment decision.
8. Keep tamper-evident, access-controlled security/audit records for issuance, trust changes, grant decisions, signatures, decryption/key-use events, saga transitions and webhook verification, without logging plaintext secrets or sensitive payloads.

## Key lifecycle proposal

* Keep root, subordinate identity/signing keys, encryption keys and data keys in separately authorized custody boundaries.
* Version keys and trust documents; support overlap during rotation, explicit expiry, emergency revocation, deny-by-default unknown key IDs and rapid propagation to every region/runtime.
* Define compromise playbooks: freeze affected identity/grants, revoke and rotate, terminate sessions/ephemeral material, quarantine ambiguous messages, replay/reconcile outboxes safely and notify affected tenants.
* Cryptographic erasure, escrow/recovery and tenant-held-key consequences require explicit policy. No algorithm, HSM/KMS vendor or dependency is selected here.

## Offline and regional constraints

Offline clients may prepare and sign/queue work but cannot treat authoritative marketplace, stock, ticket, dispatch or payment effects as confirmed. Reconnect must re-authenticate, reauthorize, check revocation/freshness and run through idempotent saga processing. Trust/revocation material needs bounded-staleness regional distribution; payload placement and key custody must respect tenant residency. Safety-sensitive degraded operation remains governed by separately approved procedures.
