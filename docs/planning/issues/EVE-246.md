## Goal

Eventiva creates supplier-owned invoices, presents buyer payment methods, deducts Eventiva commission, mirrors settlements, and manages contractual fulfilment through one provider-neutral action model. Stripe Connect is the first adapter; Airwallex, GoCardless, and later providers must be replaceable without rewriting domain, UI, agent, audit, or accounting logic.

Full specification: [Provider-neutral marketplace payments](<https://ampcode.com/artifacts/provider-neutral-marketplace-payments-8bc6fe8e-e18a-4286-8c97-219ad8e1c0ca>)

## Binding decisions

* Supplier remains seller/merchant of record through a provider-managed connected account.
* Eventiva is non-custodial: it never stores payment credentials, holds customer money, issues e-money, or presents itself as escrow.
* Stripe Connect direct-charge invoices are the first implementation. Eventiva creates them in the supplier connected-account context and takes an application fee.
* Destination/separate charges are not the default because formal disputes debit Eventiva's platform balance first.
* Eventiva owns canonical invoice, payment instruction, commission, fulfilment, refund, dispute, reconciliation, and audit records. Provider identifiers are mappings, never domain identity.
* Provider SDKs and objects stay inside integration packages behind neutral Effect services with Effect Schema contracts and typed capability errors. Domain code never branches on provider names.
* Capability discovery is per provider, merchant, country, currency, charge model, payment method, limits, refund, dispute, and payout capability. Portability does not require a lowest-common-denominator feature set.
* UI, API, MCP, ACP, webhooks, and agents use the same authorised actions. Issuing invoices, moving money, approving refunds, changing payouts, and submitting final dispute evidence require the same human approval and step-up policy across all surfaces.
* Signed webhooks are replay-protected, idempotent, retried, dead-lettered, and reconciled by provider read-back.
* No payment executes offline. Offline preparation produces drafts requiring fresh authorization and provider capability checks after reconnection.

## Required provider-neutral capabilities

1. Merchant onboarding, verification state, capabilities, disconnect/revocation.
2. Provider-scoped customer mapping from canonical Eventiva parties.
3. Invoice draft, revision, approval, issue, cancel, read-back, and externally-paid marking.
4. Payment-method discovery and provider-hosted or embedded payment sessions.
5. Commission/application fees with versioned calculation and proportional refund adjustment.
6. Refunds, disputes, evidence requirements/deadlines, settlement, payouts, and reconciliation.
7. Typed unsupported-capability outcomes rather than silent fallback.

Provider-neutral records include MerchantConnection, PaymentCustomerMapping, Invoice/InvoiceRevision, PaymentInstruction/PaymentAttempt, CommissionCharge, FulfilmentRecord, Settlement/Payout, Refund/Dispute, ProviderEventReceipt, and ReconciliationEntry.

## Core workflow

1. Supplier or authorised agent creates a canonical draft from a quote, order, event, contract, milestone, or manual entry.
2. Supplier policy controls human approval before issue.
3. Eventiva checks live merchant capabilities and creates/finalises the provider invoice under that supplier.
4. Buyer sees eligible methods for the supplier, country, currency, amount, and invoice; credentials remain with the provider.
5. Provider webhooks update local state; reconciliation resolves missed, duplicate, or reordered events.
6. Accounting receives one idempotent event for invoice, commission, fee, refund, settlement, and payout changes.

Stripe's first adapter should support eligible connected-account combinations of Invoicing, direct charges, application fees, cards, bank transfers, Pay by Bank, Bacs Direct Debit, local methods, refunds, payout state, and formal disputes. Availability is capability-driven. Bacs is asynchronous, has provider limits, and permits final indemnity claims without a fixed dispute period, so high-value invoices should prefer bank transfer or Pay by Bank where suitable.

## Fulfilment, settlement, and supplier maturity

Describe this as provider-held delayed settlement, never Eventiva escrow:

* Supplier marks an obligation or milestone fulfilled and attaches evidence.
* Buyer confirmation starts the supplier maturity delay; no buyer response permits eligibility 14 days after the contracted completion/event date when no dispute is open, with reminders first.
* A contractual dispute pauses release only where provider capabilities and agreements permit.
* Event occurrence supports but does not prove fulfilment; milestone contracts assess each accepted milestone separately.
* Every supplier starts at New. Maturity combines settled volume, completed contracts, distinct verified clients, time, fulfilment, refunds, chargebacks, disputes, verification, and compliance. Turnover alone never grants trust.

Initial calibration, kept versioned/configurable rather than hard-coded:

| Level | Qualification target | Payout policy |
| -- | -- | -- |
| New | Mandatory at onboarding; enhanced verification/provider limits | 14 days after fulfilment eligibility and slower provider payout cadence where supported |
| Established | Six months history; £50k–£100k settled; 12+ completed contracts; 2–3 distinct verified clients per qualifying month; acceptable loss/dispute rates | About seven days after fulfilment eligibility |
| Proven | Six qualifying months; £500k–£1m settled; 50+ completed contracts; around 10 distinct verified clients per qualifying month; sustained low loss/dispute rates | 72 hours after confirmation or provider's safest standard schedule |

Qualification requires target activity in at least five of six consecutive months. Maintenance uses a rolling six-month window: one missed month is grace, two gives warning/review, and three triggers proportionate demotion review. Fraud, sanctions, lapsed verification, severe disputes, or provider restrictions can pause payouts immediately. Ordinary demotion provides reasons, notice where safe, and appeal. A long-lived reliable low-turnover supplier needs an alternative evidence path.

Post-Proven Bronze/Silver/Gold or named volume badges may recognise sustained provider-verified Eventiva transaction history, but never alter payout risk, ranking, permissions, verification, or dispute treatment and never imply quality endorsement. Exact turnover is public only with supplier consent.

Eventiva contractual mediation remains separate from card, bank, Direct Debit, fraud, or provider disputes. Eventiva does not fund dispute protection at launch.

## Portability and migration

The Stripe adapter and a fake adapter must pass one conformance suite. Airwallex is a future candidate for connected multi-currency wallets, FX, global accounts, and payouts; GoCardless is a candidate for bank debit/open-banking collection where its model fits.

Switching provider routes new drafts/payments after capability validation. Historical provider mappings remain available for refunds, disputes, and reconciliation. Issued invoices and captured payments are never silently transplanted.

## Acceptance criteria

* Fake and Stripe sandbox adapters pass the same contract tests.
* One supplier-authorised invoice is drafted, approved, issued, paid, refunded, and reconciled through UI and agent surfaces using the same actions.
* At least card and one bank method work without credentials touching Eventiva.
* Sandbox evidence proves supplier merchant identity, Eventiva application fee, and liability boundaries.
* Duplicate, invalid, reordered, and missing webhooks converge to one correct result.
* Provider outage produces explicit degraded mode and never pretends money moved.
* Refunds adjust commission and accounting exactly once.
* 72-hour cooling-off, 14-day no-response eligibility, milestone release, and dispute pause are tested with a fake clock; unsupported provider holds fail safely.
* Cross-tenant access, buyer access to supplier commission/payouts, agent payment without approval, and secret/PII telemetry leakage all fail negative tests.
* Adapter replacement changes composition only; domain handlers, UI actions, invoice records, and accounting contracts remain unchanged.
