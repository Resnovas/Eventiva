## BINDING owner decision — 15 November 2026: human-in-the-loop for ALL operational emergency actions

**Never automatically actuate a show-control or operational response from an incident, alarm, emergency sensor, issue classification, agent interpretation, integration alert or rules-engine event.** An alarm/event may be ingested, classified, displayed, logged, correlated, escalated to people, propose a configured SOP or preset, and notify designated staff; it must NOT automatically GO/hold/pause/mute audio, set venue lights/DMX, trigger PA/media/announcements, start emergency scene/preset, dispatch units or carry out other consequential incident-response operations. The possibility of false alarms and resulting public panic/harm is the rationale. This is an unconditional product policy for **operational incidents and alarms**, not an admin-configurable bypass or an exception for 'tested integration'. A permissioned, accountable **human must deliberately initiate** the approved action following assessment; log initiator, confirmation, linked incident, preset/version, result, and failures. Provide quick incident-linked actions but not default-on automatic acceptance.

**Differentiate ordinary show automation:** separately programmed and human-armed cue stacks, playback sequences, lighting console input, MIDI/OSC/MSC and timecode may continue to start permitted *non-emergency show cues* according to show programming; that is not authority for automatically activating an incident/emergency response. Incoming external signals claiming to be alarms are treated as alerts for human evaluation even if received over a show-control protocol. An external operator deliberately firing an authorized ordinary cue is still allowed, subject to arming and permissions.

**System-of-systems boundary:** independent certified fire alarms, evacuation/voice alarm, emergency lighting and physical overrides retain their own mandatory automatic functions. Eventiva's human-in-the-loop restriction is for Eventiva-initiated control actions and must not inhibit certified systems, lawful fire-panel interlocks, or physical emergency interventions. Do not call for an extra Eventiva confirmation where it could delay or prevent those independent measures. Any proposed direct control of safety-certified hardware requires separate safety engineering and approval.

**Acceptance criteria:** simulate a false fire/heat alarm and a mistakenly classified crowd incident: both notify MDT with a proposed response and initiate **zero** show/lighting/audio/PA/dispatch outputs without a deliberate authorized human action; no auto approval through agents, tenant rules or remote console input marked as emergency. Authorized manual response from MDT executes only the selected versioned preset and clearly reports partial success/failure. Ordinary human-armed playback and mapped external non-emergency cue input still work independently. Test no deferred/replayed emergency output after reconnect.

Implementation and dependencies remain unapproved.

## Product decision — 15 November 2026

Eventiva must support **bidirectional operational control between the F7 control room/MDT and the separate F1 show-control/local playback system**. This is a confirmed product requirement, **not implementation, driver, dependency, listener, or deployment approval**. It is not a separate duplicate dispatch or cue engine.

### User scenarios / required UX

1. **Nightclub emergency from MDT:** an incoming live incident (e.g. crowd crush/injury on dance floor, fire, or another urgent venue issue) is received as a normal F7 issue/incident. An authorized controller can, from the incident or MDT screen, invoke a preconfigured **venue emergency-response cue/action preset**, coordinating (where safe/approved) house/work lights, music pause/stop/attenuation, PA or other audio routing, selected screens and external lighting/audio consoles. Show the incident, procedure, requested actions, individual outputs' acknowledgement/observed-state and failures in one control room; preserve fast access during stress. Response must be predefined and rehearsed, not generated ad hoc by AI.
2. **Normal cross-control:** a permitted MDT/controller action can hold/resume or GO a specific cue/group/cart, subject to live show permissions and current arming/snapshot; cue events can create/log/advance an approved F7 operational action/incident/SOP when mapped.
3. **External-to-Eventiva show control:** an incoming OSC, MIDI/MSC, timecode or supported network command from a lighting console, media server, desk or authorized control system can start an eligible Eventiva audio/video cue or cue set. Example: operator fires a cue on the lighting desk, and the mapped incoming control starts the matching sound in Eventiva.
4. **External-to-MDT operational control:** explicitly mapped incoming events from authorized external equipment/integrations can create an issue, append incident update, raise an alert, request a dispatcher action or start an SOP, and potentially make other permissioned actions when specified. No arbitrary incident closure, dispatch or privilege mutation by unmapped protocol traffic.
5. **Remote operators:** authorized crew/control staff can reach allowed MDT/show actions from their appropriate interface, including an on-site local control path when the WAN is unavailable, with honest online/reachability status. A device without a safe reachable local bridge may not promise remote control.

### Architecture boundaries

* F7 owns issues, incident triage/priority, tasks, units, assignments, control-room view, logs, handover and SOP association. F1 owns cue definitions, show modes, cue execution intent, programmed response presets and show logs. <issue id="43f1ddca-7626-469e-a568-96ae44f272eb" label="EVE-255" href="https://linear.app/resnovas/issue/EVE-255/build-separate-local-show-playback-and-cue-execution-engine" /> owns deterministic local audio/video/lighting cue playback; <issue id="96b947e4-5f8f-4905-9e99-fa55a95a0482" label="EVE-231" href="https://linear.app/resnovas/issue/EVE-231/f113-build-the-show-bridge-a-local-process-that-fires-cues-to-consoles" /> owns local bidirectional hardware/console transports; no direct F7 execution of per-frame playback and no duplicate command queues.
* A typed, auditable **control-event/mapping layer** maps incoming source events or authorized F7 commands to allowlisted inventory actions. Context: tenant, event, show run, venue, channel, source identity, actor, source sequence/timestamp, mapping version, target, intent and correlation with incident/cue. Separate a 'detected/received event' from an 'authorized command', an 'accepted command', a 'transport delivered' and a 'verified observed hardware state'.
* Show runners pin a tested response/cue snapshot and venue-specific mappings and endpoint targets. Emergency response presets are named, versioned, reversible where appropriate, and bound to the approved venue profile. No implicit global one-size-fits-all blackout/house-light action.
* Inputs are opt-in with explicit per-endpoint trust zone, credential or paired-device identity where possible, binding/mapping, per-action permissions, mode/arming rules, allowlists, freshness/replay/deduplication/rate-limit safeguards and output fencing. OSC/MIDI/DMX are not inherently authenticated; don't claim packet-level identity from those transports without a separately protected gateway. Do not expose raw OSC/MIDI/DMX listeners to public WAN by default.
* Non-safety show-control triggers may run automatically when enabled and armed according to policy. **Operational emergencies do not automatically drive safety-affecting outputs by default**; a permitted human operator selects an approved response. No exception for alarm/sensor/incident-generated automated show outputs is permitted under this product requirement; any future proposal to change that policy requires a new explicit owner decision and a separate safety review. Agents cannot self-approve emergency actions.
* Keep documented distinction between 'pause/stop entertainment audio', 'route a certified/public-address announcement', 'house/work lighting', 'certified emergency lighting/fire alarm systems' and 'physical emergency stop'. Eventiva is not the certified life-safety system; independent physical/manual controls remain. Failure/partial execution must be visible with immediate fallback instructions and responsible controller, never a false success message.
* Live execution avoids WAN/LLM dependence, does not enqueue stale GO/emergency commands for later replay, and must have tested loss-of-network, duplicate, delayed input, split-brain, device-offline, unexpected timecode, override, emergency macro conflict and recovery cases.
* Shared Agent-Native actions and screen-awareness should expose available permitted commands and outcome status to UI/MDT, APIs and authorized agents while enforcing the same permissions/approvals.

### Acceptance tests to specify and run on actual hardware

* Controller opens a nightclub incident in MDT, chooses an approved response preset; locally (with remote cloud disconnected) a test rig reports light/audio changes, shows partial/failed endpoints accurately, links the actions to incident and show logs and offers manual recovery.
* An incoming authorized lighting-console OSC/MIDI trigger launches the correct Eventiva sound cue with a unique correlation id; an unauthorized, replayed, disarmed or unmapped trigger does nothing.
* A configured external alert creates one F7 incident/action with full source and timestamp; it cannot silently dispatch units or start an emergency response without separate explicit permission/approval.
* Safety-certified systems still operate independently and are never bypassed. An unauthorized user/agent and a remote device with no live bridge cannot execute emergency presets.
* Updating a reusable show template never silently changes the armed emergency-response snapshot.
