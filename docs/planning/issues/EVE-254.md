## BINDING owner decision — 15 November 2026: human-in-the-loop for ALL operational emergency actions

**Never automatically actuate a show-control or operational response from an incident, alarm, emergency sensor, issue classification, agent interpretation, integration alert or rules-engine event.** An alarm/event may be ingested, classified, displayed, logged, correlated, escalated to people, propose a configured SOP or preset, and notify designated staff; it must NOT automatically GO/hold/pause/mute audio, set venue lights/DMX, trigger PA/media/announcements, start emergency scene/preset, dispatch units or carry out other consequential incident-response operations. The possibility of false alarms and resulting public panic/harm is the rationale. This is an unconditional product policy for **operational incidents and alarms**, not an admin-configurable bypass or an exception for 'tested integration'. A permissioned, accountable **human must deliberately initiate** the approved action following assessment; log initiator, confirmation, linked incident, preset/version, result, and failures. Provide quick incident-linked actions but not default-on automatic acceptance.

**Differentiate ordinary show automation:** separately programmed and human-armed cue stacks, playback sequences, lighting console input, MIDI/OSC/MSC and timecode may continue to start permitted *non-emergency show cues* according to show programming; that is not authority for automatically activating an incident/emergency response. Incoming external signals claiming to be alarms are treated as alerts for human evaluation even if received over a show-control protocol. An external operator deliberately firing an authorized ordinary cue is still allowed, subject to arming and permissions.

**System-of-systems boundary:** independent certified fire alarms, evacuation/voice alarm, emergency lighting and physical overrides retain their own mandatory automatic functions. Eventiva's human-in-the-loop restriction is for Eventiva-initiated control actions and must not inhibit certified systems, lawful fire-panel interlocks, or physical emergency interventions. Do not call for an extra Eventiva confirmation where it could delay or prevent those independent measures. Any proposed direct control of safety-certified hardware requires separate safety engineering and approval.

**Acceptance criteria:** simulate a false fire/heat alarm and a mistakenly classified crowd incident: both notify MDT with a proposed response and initiate **zero** show/lighting/audio/PA/dispatch outputs without a deliberate authorized human action; no auto approval through agents, tenant rules or remote console input marked as emergency. Authorized manual response from MDT executes only the selected versioned preset and clearly reports partial success/failure. Ordinary human-armed playback and mapped external non-emergency cue input still work independently. Test no deferred/replayed emergency output after reconnect.

Implementation and dependencies remain unapproved.

## Owner clarification — bidirectional MDT/show control, 15 November 2026

The approved requirement is **not just to notify show operators of incidents**: a permitted F7 MDT/controller must be able to invoke **preconfigured show cues and venue emergency-response presets** from an active incident (e.g. bring on approved work lights, pause entertainment audio, coordinate external consoles), with the F1 show engine as the only playback/output executor. Inbound authorized console/media-server OSC/MIDI/MSC/timecode/network events must be able to target permitted F1 cues, and mapped external events may drive approved F7 alerts/issues/SOPs. <issue id="e6838c83-005e-4ec5-bc2f-931de6cd0cb8" label="EVE-256" href="https://linear.app/resnovas/issue/EVE-256/connect-mdt-incidents-and-external-controls-to-live-show-cues-and" /> owns the cross-module command/event mappings and tests. Incidents must not automatically fire safety-affecting outputs just because of a classification; human-invoked approved preset is the default. Independently certified venue safety systems remain independent. Product scope confirmed; implementation separately gated.

## BINDING owner scope decision — 2026-11-15: complete QLab-class capability, separate local playback module

The owner explicitly selected **native playback AND external control** (resolves the former A/B question in favour of A), with the complete QLab-inspired capability families as product requirements: audio, mic/live audio, video/stills, camera/live video, text, lighting, fades, network, MIDI, MIDI file, timecode and programmable/script cues, plus cue lists, groups/nested sequences, timelines, cue carts and operator transport/control cues (GO, stop/pause, wait, load/reset, goto/target, arm/disarm, etc). The benchmark includes high-quality routing, effects, synchronization, rehearsal preview, cue grouping/timing and repeatable show programming; **feature parity is a design/acceptance target, not a claim of present implementation nor a licence to copy proprietary QLab code/UI**. Exact engine libraries/codecs and support by OS still require proof and dependency approval.

**Separate modules, one production:** native audio/video/media playback and deterministic real-time device output belong in a **dedicated local show-engine/playback module**, NOT inside the F7 MDT/CAD dispatch module. F7 owns incoming operational issues/requests, incidents, unit dispatch, status, comms and escalations. F1 owns event phases, production plans, cue authoring/calling, rehearsal, production logs and publication. A common versioned action/event/readiness model coordinates both, using the same Eventiva identities, tenant isolation, approvals, audit, screen awareness and agent-native interface. Live cue output never depends on the cloud or a non-deterministic LLM and must not share the MDT dispatch queue as its execution path.

**Repeatability is first-class and must be easy for operators:** reusable show templates, reusable hierarchical cue sets/palettes/carts, versioned cue libraries, device/output/venue patch profiles, scene/sequence templates, per-show instances with safe overrides, dry-run/preflight, rehearsal/test and immutable published/armed show snapshots. Tour/repeat-date templates carry cue/media assets and a dependency/asset manifest, relative timings and venue mappings; live runs pin exact versions, with explicit review/rehearsal/republish when source templates change. Workspaces should not silently mutate a published show when a shared reusable cue set is edited. Preserve F1.15 template/cloning semantics without copying live runs, approvals or rigging sign-offs.

**Bidirectional event handling:** outbound integrations remain planned (OSC, MIDI/MSC, timecode, Art-Net/sACN and adapters for external QLab/media servers/consoles). Native show engine also needs **incoming external control and time signals** (e.g., OSC, MIDI/MSC, MTC/LTC and supported network triggers) that can drive eligible cue actions through authenticated/authorized routes with replay/rate/input validation. Independently, incoming real issues, requests, live incidents and operations/status events must be visible to show operators and linked to cue hold/escalation SOPs, without automatically firing safety-critical outputs. The owner's words "handling the incoming" should be clarified in context; do not assume which incoming signals or platform events should trigger automated GO.

**Scripting:** programmable cue behavior is in scope, but an unsandboxed script must never gain system shell, credentials, privileged control or side-effect access beyond allowed Eventiva actions/driver ports. Script language/runtime, portability, deterministic behavior, execution budgets, review/signing and trust policy require a separately approved design and dependency set.

**Safety:** separate plan/edit, rehearsal/test, live/go and postshow modes; protect external outputs in simulation; preserve explicit human arming, permission boundaries, operator ownership/fencing, emergency stop, qualified rigging sign-off and certified external safety systems. Agents can build, test, explain and propose cues but may only act within delegated live permissions/approvals and no auto-generated emergency logic. Retain functioning Community single-host local playback in offline/degraded situations.

**Implementation status and authorization:** this records a **confirmed product scope decision only**. It does not approve any dependency/driver/codec, image, new executable, OS-specific device support, paid service, network listener exposure or code delivery yet. Further design should produce a capability by cue type/protocol and a staged test/rollout matrix. ## Owner goal (2026-11-15)

Eventiva should serve **the entire live production lifecycle**, from advance, rigging and pre-show construction through installation, commissioning, rehearsal, every show cue/task/incident/emergency, and derig/load-out and reporting, within **one shared event and control system**. QLab ([https://qlab.app/](<https://qlab.app/>)) is a primary **functional and operator-workflow benchmark**, not source code/UI/assets to copy. This is a product-direction/specification and gap-review issue, **not implementation authorization**.

Cue models with stable ids/number, typed targets, list/cart/group/timeline, trigger, delay, repeat, duration, output routing, preflight/readiness, state and audit. Rehearsal/preview/test is isolated from live outputs. Published live cue package is immutable, changes are versioned and qualified. Operator modes: plan/program, rehearse, show-control/GO and postshow audit; functional UI/API/agents share actions and screen projections.

Critical architecture: run physical media and device outputs in a **deterministic local show executor**, capable of running through WAN/cloud loss. Effect Cluster/Workflow coordinates authorization, shared state and durable history, but does not sit in per-frame playback or low-latency signal generation. Local ownership/fencing and safety-tested handoff must prevent competing GO or duplicated device effects. Distinguish hold/pause/panic/stop/failsafe and certified emergency equipment. Human-only arming, protected sign-off, manual takeover and fail-closed authorization; agents may draft/preview/analyze and perform only explicitly delegated authorized actions. Versioned procedures cannot invent emergency/safety steps. Preserve self-hosted Community and independent physical backup controls.

End-to-end acceptance scenarios: a staged build from drawing and task dependency to signed-off rigging; a rehearsal cue stack with manual/auto-follow, groups/cart and controlled outputs; a mixed audio/video/lighting cue whose physical outcomes and timestamp accuracy are validated; missed cue/failed output, media missing, WAN disconnection, clock jump, process crash, competing controllers; an incident prompts documented safe operational response and handover without fabricating safety controls; derig returns equipment, raises defects and generates postshow reports. Test on real playback/hardware before claiming QLab-class reliability.

## One major scope decision remaining

**A — Native QLab-style playback:** Eventiva ultimately plays/renders audio/video and other media itself through its local executor while supporting external consoles and QLab interoperability.
**B — Orchestration-first only:** Eventiva contains cue programming/GO and all lifecycle control but delegates actual media playback to QLab, consoles and media servers.

Determine this before locking implementation layers, media dependencies or execution roadmap. Do not silently restrict the owner's 'replicate QLab' ambition to sending OSC triggers. Every new dependency/version, native runtime, host platform and output hardware test requires the usual explicit gate. Existing F1.13 D1=C (protocol triggers + direct DMX for small rigs) remains approved and is not automatically rescinded by this proposed expansion.
