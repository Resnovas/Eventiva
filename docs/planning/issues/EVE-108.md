## 1. Goal

An organiser draws an accurate, to-scale map of any site once (an outdoor field or festival, an arena, a conference centre, a theatre, a custom-built structure, or a blank plan with no street map at all), plans every event phase on it with the real equipment, vehicles, gates and people it uses, shares it safely with crew, authorities and attendees, and watches it live on the day, on web, desktop and mobile, offline where it can be.

## 2. Users and jobs

| User | Jobs |
| -- | -- |
| Organiser / production manager | Create a site from satellite imagery or an uploaded plan; lay out stages, tents, fencing, toilets, power, routes and zones per phase (build, show, break); check capacity and clashes; issue revisions for approval; print at scale. |
| Venue manager | Keep a reusable, multi-level plan of their building (walls, rooms, doors, stairs, lifts, seating bowls, services) that every event at the venue starts from. |
| Site crew and stewards | See the layers for their role on a phone, offline; find their post and the nearest facility; report where something actually is. |
| Vendors and contractors | See their plot and its constraints; place their own items inside their plot only. |
| Safety officer and authorities (fire, police, council) | Review an issued revision through a view-only, expiring link; comment; approve or reject. |
| Attendees | Use a published public map in the app or on the event website for wayfinding and facilities, offline once loaded. |
| Finance and logistics | Read the bill of quantities (fence metres, item counts) generated from the plan to drive hire, picking and costs. |
| Agent | Do everything a person can through the same actions: draft a layout from a brief, place and move items, compute quantities and capacity, flag clashes, answer "where is X", and prepare revisions for a person to issue. |

Positions: F10 stores no positions. F7 (<issue id="81e5a26e-791e-4891-bb97-c481901f11d7" label="EVE-181" href="https://linear.app/resnovas/issue/EVE-181/f710-build-live-unit-positions-on-the-event-map-with-consent-and" />, `ops.position.report`) is Eventiva's single location collector for units and opted-in rostered crew; F10 shows them through live layer sources (agreed with F7 on 2026-11-01). As-built reports from a phone carry the reported accuracy radius: phone GPS is about 5 m in open sky and unreliable indoors, so a report worse than the tenant setting (default 10 m) is flagged for review instead of moving the feature, and indoor reports pick a room or level instead of a GPS point.

Documents embedded in records (<issue id="a1348819-43cc-4005-8e8d-a896a761af03" label="EVE-51" href="https://linear.app/resnovas/issue/EVE-51/p112-build-collaboration-yjs-for-text-and-canvas-with-record-sync-and" />): each MapLayer owns one Yjs document, so authorization at sync is per layer and a vendor never receives another vendor's plot. A SiteMap is the record that versions, permissions and syncs its layer documents; a revision freezes all of them together.

Coordinates: every Site has a site-local frame (origin at the site anchor, x east and y north, in metres). Feature geometry is stored in that frame, which is the contract F1 and others place elements in; pure conversion functions to and from WGS84 live in the shared contracts package and are used for rendering on imagery and for GeoJSON export. Indoor or unsurveyed plans are calibrated into the same frame. A `plan` site works entirely in that frame with no basemap, and can be anchored to a real location later without moving any feature.

Venues and buildings (<issue id="7a4bb64d-c3c5-44aa-9209-9fc139348225" label="EVE-238" href="https://linear.app/resnovas/issue/EVE-238/f1012-venue-and-building-plans-arenas-halls-custom-structures-and" />): building elements are feature kinds (wall, room, door or opening, column, stair, ramp, lift, escalator, seating section, arena bowl, stage, rigging point, service point, loading dock); stairs, ramps, lifts and escalators are vertical connectors between levels; any footprint feature (marquee, building, container stage) can open its own interior child map. An F1 Space links to a room or zone by feature id.

External layer registry: other modules register `external` (record-backed) or `live` (stream-backed) layer sources at activation; F10 renders them and applies audience rules, the owning module owns the data (F10.2). Public ids for other modules: SiteMap, MapLayer, MapFeature, and Zone (a Zone id is its feature id).

## 7. Agent behaviour

When asked:

* "Lay out the show phase for 8,000 standing": `layout.draft` from the brief and the tenant rule set onto a new draft layer, then `check.run`, then present quantities; applying needs the person's confirm.
* "Move the bar 20 m north", "add a 2 m fence along the east boundary", "where is generator 3": the matching feature actions or `sitemap.find`.
* "Is the main arena over capacity?" or "do we have enough toilets?": `zone.define` results, `check.run` and the facility ratios in the rule set, compared with F8 ticket sales.
* "Send the plan to the council": prepare `revision.issue` and `share.create`; both need a person's approval.

Proactively (behind the agent's notification settings, never acting without being asked):

* After any accepted edit batch, run checks and surface new blocking findings (blocked emergency route, item outside boundary, zone over capacity).
* When F8 sales or F1 attendance forecasts change, flag zones and facility ratios that no longer meet the rule set.

## 11. Compliance

* Personal data: crew positions are personal data (UK GDPR and EU GDPR; employee monitoring guidance). F10 collects and stores none; consent, retention (30 days), the DPIA and purge live with F7's single collector (<issue id="81e5a26e-791e-4891-bb97-c481901f11d7" label="EVE-181" href="https://linear.app/resnovas/issue/EVE-181/f710-build-live-unit-positions-on-the-event-map-with-consent-and" />). F10 applies viewer rules and never sends attendee location to the server.
* Crowd capacity and facility ratios are planning aids, not certification. Default rule sets cite their source and hold only figures the source publishes openly or the tenant enters. The Purple Guide is subscription-only, so its starter rules hold the ratios from its public sanitation introduction (one female toilet per 100 attendees, one male toilet per 500 plus one urinal per 150, at least one accessible toilet) and the tenant adds the rest from their own copy. The Green Guide is a sports-ground guide whose safe capacity is the lowest of holding, entry, exit and emergency exit capacity, adjusted by its P and S factors, so zones compute holding capacity, checks compute exit capacity from route widths and the tenant's flow rates, and the zone shows the lower figure. US starter rules use the occupant load factors the jurisdiction adopts (IBC Table 1004.5 or NFPA 101 Table 7.3.1.2, for example 7 net sq ft per person for chairs only and 5 for standing space). Every capacity figure in exports carries "calculated estimate, verify with your safety adviser".
* Map data licences: OpenStreetMap data needs ODbL attribution on screen and on prints; imagery vendors' terms on caching, printing and offline use apply and are checked before choosing (D-F10-1, D-F10-6). Verified 2026-11-08 with [MapLibre React Native v11 notes](<https://maplibre.org/maplibre-react-native/docs/setup/migrations/v11>), [MapLibre Native PMTiles](<https://maplibre.org/maplibre-native/android/examples/data/PMTiles/>), [Protomaps downloads](<https://docs.protomaps.com/basemaps/downloads>), [Mapbox offline terms](<https://docs.mapbox.com/android/maps/guides/offline/concepts/>), [Esri imagery export](<https://www.esri.com/arcgis-blog/products/arcgis-living-atlas/imagery/wayback-export>), [MapTiler data plans](<https://maptiler.com/data/pricing/>), [Yjs map conflict discussion](<https://discuss.yjs.dev/t/y-map-conflict-resolution-which-side-wins/1952>), [SGSA safe capacity](<https://sgsa.org.uk/calculating-safe-capacity>), [Purple Guide sanitation](<https://www.thepurpleguide.co.uk/sanitation>), [Martyn's Law summary](<https://www.protectuk.police.uk/sites/default/files/2025-09/31.187_HO_Martyns%20Law_1_pager_Web_1.pdf>), [IMDF OGC standard](<https://docs.ogc.org/cs/20-094/>).
* Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA. The map is not the only way to get information: the POI list, quantities table and findings list are text equivalents; keyboard placement and movement in the editor; colour is never the only legend signal.
* Share links: tokens hashed at rest, expiry required (default 30 days), password optional, revocation immediate.
* Martyn's Law (UK Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025, passed 3 April 2025, implementation period of at least 24 months, regulated by the SIA): premises and events for 200 to 799 people are standard tier and 800 or more are enhanced tier, and enhanced-tier events must document their public protection measures. The `ops` layer kind carries evacuation, invacuation and lockdown routes and points and exports with an issued revision; statutory guidance is not out yet, so no export format is fixed.
