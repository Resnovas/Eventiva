## Goal

Vendors send priced, versioned quotes. The organiser compares requirements and products side by side, then either accepts a complete supplier quote or builds one mixed-supplier award plan selecting individual offered lines. Each awarded supplier receives a separate contract, booking/capacity commitment and payment schedule; no quote is silently divided into new legally binding prices.

## Decision D5-7: mixed-supplier awards (2026-11-13)

## Decision D5-8 - progressive comparison and planning-driven quote impact (2026-11-13)

### Goal

Make complex supplier comparisons usable from a stage-level overview down to the exact quoted product or service, while keeping supplier quote validity aligned with the organiser's changing event requirements. This extends D5-7 whole-quote and mixed-supplier awards and the attached requirement-level comparison specification; it does not replace those contracts.

### Progressive disclosure and visual status

* Default overview presents event areas such as Stage 1, Stage 2, Stage 3, or the equivalent organiser-defined plan hierarchy. Show comparable cost roll-ups, supplier coverage, quotation freshness and a small, non-intrusive issue indicator. Do not force the full equipment matrix on initial load.
* Drill path: event -> stage/area -> category or discipline (audio, lighting, video, etc.) -> requirement -> individual proposed product/service, bundle components, evidence and quote revision differences. Support navigating back without losing filters, selection or context.
* Derive parent warning state and counts from affected descendants. Use concise accessible icons and labels, and avoid flooding the overview with per-line warnings. Provide filters such as 'show issues only' and clear visual distinctions for 'not supplied', 'not quoted', 'not applicable', 'non-equivalent', 'partially covered', 'price anomaly', 'outdated quote', 'awaiting supplier confirmation', and 'resolved'. Never rely on colour, grey-out or strikethrough alone. Suppressed/non-applicable lines must remain accessible for audit.
* Coverage is defined relative to current requirement versions, quantities, units and technical criteria. Do not treat an item as covered merely because a supplier used a similar product name. Multi-line kits and functional-equivalence approvals must roll up without double-counting prices or coverage.
* Major deficiencies (e.g. half the required audio equipment missing) show a conspicuous but restrained deficiency count or coverage summary at stage level; reveal exact missing lines upon drill-down. Percentages must be based on a disclosed denominator, not an unexplained score.
* Preserve accessibility, keyboard navigation, screen-reader status, responsive layouts and offline access to the most recently synchronised comparison, clearly labelled if stale. Large comparisons should load detail incrementally without requiring full materialisation in the initial view.

### Planning-to-sourcing dependency and change impact

* The F1 event plan/production requirements own the authoritative versioned requirement tree, including stable requirement ID, stage/category linkage, quantity, units, specification, timing and constraints. F5 comparisons and supplier quote lines reference requirement IDs and the exact requirement revision they were priced against. Integrate with F1/F5.7/F5.8 and F6 inventory requirements rather than creating a second source of truth.
* When a requirement changes, emit a durable, idempotent change event and recompute affected coverage, equivalence, pricing assumptions, selection plans and supplier response status. Changes to quantity, product, specifications, dates, venue/stage or removed/added requirements must be distinguishable.
* Example: Stage 2 vocal microphone requirement changes from 20 to 10. Existing supplier quotes for 20 are preserved as immutable supplier versions and flagged 'needs review / quoted against earlier requirement' with before/after quantities. A unit-price multiplication may be shown only as a clearly labelled non-binding estimate when valid; discounts, minimum hire quantities, bundle pricing, freight and crew impacts require supplier reconfirmation. Do not silently rewrite or accept supplier quotes.
* Scope impact detection traverses bundles, shared equipment, staffing, hire dates, dependencies, substitutions and mixed-supplier selections. A changed requirement need not invalidate unrelated areas or quotes. The system should distinguish automatic clear matches from uncertain impacts needing human review.
* Buyer can review impact, approve suggested supplier clarification, request revised quotes, compare revisions and accept or dismiss an issue with reason. Supplier receives only their own affected requirement changes and approved communication, never competitors' prices. Maintain immutable history, actor, time, previous/new requirement versions, impacted quote IDs, supplier responses and final resolution.
* Already accepted supplier contracts and commitments must not be edited by a changed plan: create a change-request/amendment workflow with authorised approval, supplier confirmation and independent accounting/booking adjustments. Agent tools use the same authorised operations as UI and cannot auto-commit changes or contact suppliers without the requisite permissions and approval.
* Offline plan edits may queue and sync, but stale comparisons cannot be represented as current; reconcile concurrent planning edits with version checks and conflict handling. Recompute asynchronously and show pending/stale indicators when needed without blocking the planning UI.

### Validation

1. On opening a comparison covering three stages, only stage-level summary rows render initially, with correct totals, coverage and issue count; selecting Stage 2, then Audio, then Microphones exposes individual supplier lines without loss of context.
2. A supplier omitting 50% of the defined Stage 2 audio requirements produces a visible deficiency indication at Stage 2 and Audio, with exact missing requirements identifiable on expansion. 'Not applicable' and 'not supplied' never merge into a misleading status.
3. Changing a requirement from 20 microphones to 10 marks only affected supplier quotes and award selections stale, preserves prior sent quote versions and prices, and offers a supplier re-quote workflow; no binding price, contract or invoice changes automatically.
4. Supplier bundles covering more than one requirement are represented without duplicated monetary totals; changing one bundled requirement flags the whole price dependency when it cannot be safely separated.
5. A previously accepted contract remains unchanged until a separately approved amendment. Failed change processing is retryable, auditable and cannot duplicate notifications, commitments or awards.
6. The same impact details and navigation are available through Agent-Native actions to appropriately authorised agents, with cross-supplier confidentiality and negative permission tests.
7. Mobile/web, screen-reader, keyboard, offline/stale-data and large-comparison performance tests prove the summary remains usable and warnings are perceivable beyond colour.

### Out of scope / escalation

This decision does not authorise auto-awarding, auto-renegotiation or an unapproved redesign of the F1 planning schema. Before implementation, align F1 requirement versioning and event production planning issue ownership, plus existing F5.8 detailed document/action contracts. Escalate ambiguity around requirement-tree ownership, mixed-unit coverage denominators, discounts or bundled repricing rather than inventing values. Planning only.

## Decision D5-9 - multi-round supplier quote redlines and discount regression detection (2026-11-13)

### Goal

Organisers can compare successive quotation rounds from the same supplier, separately from cross-supplier comparisons, and identify additions, removals, specification changes and unexpected price changes without manually rereading every line. Detect the real-world failure in which a supplier revises a quote and an agreed discounted price silently returns to its default rate.

### Context and scope

* A sourcing exercise has requirement revisions and optional named quotation rounds (Round 1, Round 2, Round 3); each supplier has its own immutable sequence of sent quote versions, timestamp, attached original proposal and supplier identity. Round membership is explicit, and a supplier may submit multiple versions within one round or decline a round. Do not assume all suppliers submit simultaneously.
* Provide two distinct comparisons: (a) supplier A revision N versus supplier A revision N-1 or any selected historical baseline; (b) supplier A, B and C against a common requirement revision in a selected round. Provide a toggle to compare against the last quote, first quote, latest agreed commercial baseline or a user-selected version. Preserve requirement-version differences when comparing changed criteria.
* Match line items by durable supplier line identity, product/catalogue identity and requirement identity, using explicit human-confirmed continuity when supplier identifiers are regenerated, descriptions change, items split/merge, bundles are restructured or products are substituted. Matching confidence is not proof; ambiguous matches must be marked for review. Deleted lines and unpriced additions remain visible in the redline and audit trail.
* Every change receives a typed, accessible label: added, removed, quantity/unit changed, product/substitution changed, scope changed, unit price changed, discount changed, tax changed, fee changed, bundle repriced, total changed, and unchanged. Show old/new values, absolute and percentage deltas when mathematically meaningful; distinguish unit-price change from quantity-driven extended-total change, and include all applicable taxes/fees with currency and FX basis indicated. Avoid misleading percentages when a baseline is zero or prices are opaque.
* Persist the agreed or supplier-confirmed commercial pricing baseline per stable line or negotiated package: price basis, list price if explicitly supplied, agreed net unit/package rate, discount type/value, currency, quantity tiers, validity/effective dates, and supplier confirmation/evidence. Distinguish a negotiated rate from an inferred discount. Negotiated rates may change legitimately when the scope, volume or validity changes; do not assume rates are perpetual.
* Flag any unchanged or comparably matched line whose new net unit rate exceeds an applicable previously confirmed negotiated rate, particularly where it appears to revert to list/default price. A potentially expired or quantity-conditioned discount is flagged as 'requires confirmation', not automatically labelled supplier error. Highlight unexpected discount removal even if another line reduces the overall quote total. Do not auto-correct supplier-owned quote versions, invent discounts, or declare a binding agreed price without evidence.
* For a revised round, split overall financial movement into at least: quantities, net unit prices/discounts, added/removed scope, bundle/fee effects, tax, and unallocated/unexplained residual. The decomposition must reconcile to the old/new totals on a like-for-like currency/tax basis; where decomposition is impossible, mark it unknown rather than invent numbers. Whole proposal totals and stage/category roll-ups reflect these differences.
* In the stage-first progressive view established by D5-8, show a subtle summary such as '3 changes, 1 price regression' or 'discount needs confirmation'; drill through stage -> category -> line to see old/new quotes and supplier evidence. Filters include 'changes only', 'price increases', 'discount regressions' and 'unresolved'. Explicit icons/labels must work without relying on colour alone.
* Enable the organiser to acknowledge a change, flag a suspected regression, attach notes/evidence, request supplier clarification or re-quote, mark a variance accepted with an authorised reason, and retain all resolutions across later rounds. Suppliers see only their own quote and approved requests, never competing supplier commercial terms. Agent-Native actions expose the same history and review workflow under equivalent permissions; no autonomous acceptance or supplier messaging without relevant approval.
* Quote revisions are supplier-submitted immutable snapshots; round/comparison results and human review notes are separately versioned. Changes after contract acceptance require supplier amendment/approval, not mutation of an executed commitment. Support offline review of cached versions marked stale, but sending and accepting require online checks and current authorisation.

### Validation and example

* Illustrative: Round 1 quotes 20 microphones at a supplier-confirmed discounted net rate of GBP 40 each (GBP 800 pre-tax). Round 2 still quotes 20 identical microphones but unit rate silently becomes GBP 50 (GBP 1,000). Display +GBP 10/unit, +GBP 200 total and +25% with 'possible discounted-rate regression', even if the total quotation fell because unrelated lines were removed. Historical discount evidence is linked and the user's review remains unresolved until explicitly addressed.
* When requirements change from 20 to 10 microphones and the quoted rate changes, show quantity movement separately from price movement; check whether the confirmed rate applied at 10 units before labelling a regression. Never assume the total must be half.
* One line split into two lines or combined into a supplier kit cannot disappear from history or double-count money; incomplete matching is flagged for manual confirmation.
* Adding/removing items, scope substitutions, tax/fee changes and the complete round-by-round comparison are testable. Selection of any historical baseline reproduces the same deltas. Quote history, agreements, evidence, notes and approvals remain immutable/auditable.
* A three-supplier two-round exercise shows both each supplier's own revision history and current cross-supplier coverage on the same requirements, with no leakage of confidential prices.

### Boundaries and escalation

Use the F5 quote/QuoteLine versioning and the requirement-level comparison model already specified; integrate F1 requirements, F6 catalogue and F11 commitments without creating parallel commercial sources of truth. Supplier-confirmed discounts and packages must be distinguished from assumptions or calculator estimates. Escalate ambiguity over tier pricing, currencies, partial kits, matching, contract amendments and expired concessions. Planning only; do not implement or send supplier requests yet.

## Decision D5-10 - neutral bidirectional change markers and quick review (2026-11-13)

User correction to D5-9: any material price difference, whether increase **or decrease**, should carry the same neutral visual 'Changed - review' indication. A change is not inherently an error or a warning; show direction and amount separately from status. Never use a red icon simply because a unit price rose, nor a green icon simply because it fell. Reserve escalated severity for separately evidenced risks (e.g. violated supplier-confirmed pricing baseline, missing mandatory scope, incompatibility).

* Mark both price increases and reductions across quote rounds, even when total quote movement masks them. Compare quantities, unit prices, discounts, line totals and applicable tax/fees; separate price and quantity causes as specified in D5-9.
* Default UI: compact neutral change icon and accessible 'Changed' label in overview, stage/category roll-ups and line-level detail; drill down to old value -> new value with signed difference, percentage where valid, source quote revision and change reason if known. Changes remain discoverable without reliance on colour.
* Provide single-action 'Reviewed / Dismiss' for ordinary differences without requiring a note or multistep dialogue. Keep an option to add a note or request clarification. Dismissing clears the pending attention indicator, **not** the immutable record of the underlying difference. Allow reopening, and track reviewer, time, compared versions and optional reason. A subsequent new revision creates a fresh review item; no permanent suppression of future changes.
* Distinguish 'unreviewed change', 'reviewed change', and 'verified issue' as separate concepts; flagging an unexpected price regression remains possible, but not every increase automatically becomes a regression. Drops can be favourable or can indicate missing scope, changed specification, removed labour or a quotation mistake, so they merit the same quick check.
* Configurable materiality thresholds may reduce noise in top-level roll-ups but must never hide line-level changes in an explicit complete-diff view; no threshold value is assumed.

Acceptance: two otherwise identical revisions where an item goes GBP 40 -> GBP 50 and GBP 50 -> GBP 40 both show equivalent neutral change markers and accurate signed deltas. Reviewer can dismiss either with one action; overview attention count drops while change history remains accessible, auditable and visible in full diff. Changes in quantity or bundle scope are separately explained. An independently confirmed breach can still be escalated without treating all price increases as errors. Same actions and permissions apply to human and agent interfaces. Planning only.

## Decision D5-11 - hybrid planning propagation with manual supplier re-quote release (2026-11-13)

### Goal and real workflow

Internal changes to event specifications must immediately propagate as **impact information** to sourcing and quote comparisons, while supplier communication and commercial changes remain deliberate. Event planning may be exploratory (e.g. leadership proposes scope before a sale is secured); drafting many changes must not bombard suppliers with repeated re-quote requests. Product planning decision: **hybrid**, not automatic supplier outreach and not fully manual change discovery.

### Automatic internal propagation

* F1 remains the owner of versioned event requirement records. Every draft or approved requirement revision produces an internal change event with durable requirement ID, revision, provenance, author/time and correlation to the event, stage and sourcing package. Other modules update derived views, not the authoritative F1 requirement.
* Mark affected supplier quote lines, coverage, equivalence, stage/category totals and previous comparison baselines as 'review against revised requirement' or 'potentially outdated'. Detect incompatibilities and gaps as soon as the requirement changes; do not treat flags as supplier-confirmed mismatches or rewrite the supplier's immutable quote.
* Update internal forecasts, planning budget scenarios and procurement impact lists as appropriate, distinguishing exploratory vs approved scenario numbers, and preserving accepted contractual prices. Show a unified, unobtrusive change-impact queue across quotes, supplier enquiries, allocations and commitments; distinguish 'changed but not sent', 'supplier action requested', 'waiting for revised quote', 'resolved', and 'requires contract amendment'.
* Flag impacted stock/reservations and scheduling implications for review. Never silently release confirmed reservations or modify purchase orders, signed contracts, approved commitments or actual accounting entries.

### Explicit supplier action gate

* A planning change **never automatically** sends a supplier an updated brief, re-quote request, cancellation, revised contract or commercial instruction. Such externally consequential actions require deliberate authorised release of a selected, version-locked change set after manual review. This applies equally to humans and agents using the same Agent-Native action/permission/approval path.
* Users can classify event ideas, proposals or scenarios as **exploratory / not for procurement**, and can defer a specific quote revision indefinitely, until a sale, scope approval or other business milestone. These can continue changing and being costed internally without supplier notifications.
* Support collecting multiple revisions into one pending change set, previewing the cumulative delta against the last brief *actually sent to that supplier*, selecting relevant supplier(s) and requirements, and releasing **one** consolidated re-quote when ready. Do not compare only to the most recent draft; separate 'latest plan' from 'supplier has seen version'.
* Before release show the old supplier-visible brief, the proposed replacement, added/removed/changed requirements, exclusions, quote impact, recipient(s), any outstanding dependencies and optional message. User can edit, split, hold, dismiss, or approve the selected batch. Approval records actor/authority, time, exact version, recipients and released content. Dispatch is idempotent, retries do not send duplicates, failures are visible.
* Concurrent changes after user approval must not silently join an already-approved transmission. If further changes occur while a release is pending, preserve them as new unsent differences and require re-review before another outbound communication. If a batch has changed between preview and approval, block stale approval and ask for refreshed review.
* Support explicit 'do not request re-quote yet', 'waiting for sale', 'on hold', and later 'ready to send' states without automated deadlines or forced sends. Reminders may be configured separately, but no default auto-send after a timer/debounce.
* Supplier replies link to a specific released request package, supplier, requirement baseline and quote revision. Preserve every round and use the existing D5-9/D5-10 neutral bidirectional change markers and one-action internal acknowledgement; acknowledgement of a price difference is **not** permission to contact a supplier.

### State/authority model

* Distinguish internal event plan versions, procurement-released requirement baseline per supplier, quote revisions received, winning award plan, and executed contract baselines. This prevents conceptual proposals from being mistaken for purchased scope.
* Approval to release sourcing requests and approval to award/contract are separate entitlements and transitions. Agents can prepare impact summaries, draft requests and suggest consolidation, but cannot bypass release approval, signer authority, revocation or supplier isolation.
* Consider approval policies configurable by organisation/event, including delegation to an authorised procurement role; do not presume only one named individual can approve, and do not assert a sale is always required for all event types.

### Verification examples

* An exploratory change from 20 microphones to 10 marks existing quotes potentially outdated and updates internal scenario comparisons immediately; no supplier is contacted and original supplier quote totals stay immutable.
* A further change ten minutes later adjusts that same scope and adds lighting. The organiser sees cumulative changes from the last supplier-visible brief, with a single optional re-quote request after approval, not two automatic emails.
* If the sale falls through, the changes can remain exploratory, be cancelled or retained as scenarios; nothing has been transmitted or contracted.
* A released Round 2 request links to a fixed requirement snapshot. Changes made while a send is awaiting approval require re-review. A supplier Round 2 response is compared against Round 1 and the scope sent in Round 2; discount regressions and both price increases/decreases are tracked without treating every difference as an error.
* Signed commitments remain unchanged until a separately approved amendment; audit log reconstructs who saw, approved and sent exactly which change set and when.

### Integration and boundaries

## Decision D5-12 - supplier-by-supplier re-quote selection, multi-supplier batches and flexible scope views (2026-11-13)

### Goal

Organisers and tenants can run procurement in the way their event works: request revisions from one selected supplier or several at once; obtain quotes covering a single technical discipline or multiple disciplines; and compare, combine, group or split those supplier quotes into whatever view helps them make a decision. A supplier's submitted quotation and commercial terms remain the immutable source record regardless of how they are displayed.

### Supplier selection and approval

* In the internal review queue from D5-11, show an independent selection control per eligible supplier, with select-all/selected-subset and one-at-a-time operation. * Store immutable source proposal/version, document/attachments, supplier-defined hierarchy, lines and totals with provenance. ## Decision D5-13 - exact-model versus function-based requirements, supplier alternatives and contextual equivalence (2026-11-13)

### Goal and confirmed choice

The organiser can choose, **per individual event requirement line**, whether procurement must match an exact specified product/model (e.g. performer rider) or may accept supplier-recommended products that achieve an agreed function (e.g. one handheld microphone for speech). Supplier suggestions are welcome for functional requirements and can also be explicitly submitted as **non-compliant alternatives** to exact requirements, without silently being presented as compliant. Comparison should put functionally acceptable offers together even when manufacturers and models differ.

### Core model and buyer UX

* F1/<issue id="a58abd97-6c1f-4235-a66f-e5e161edebc7" label="EVE-229" href="https://linear.app/resnovas/issue/EVE-229/f111-build-equipment-requirements-roll-up-placed-elements-and-hand" /> owns the authoritative, versioned requirement. Add a required fulfilment policy at line level: **Exact item** or **Functional / equivalent allowed**; allow a sensible tenant/category default but an explicit override per requirement. Do not flatten complex stage/rider specifications into a single exact/generic flag across the entire quote.
* Exact: record manufacturer, specific model/variant and applicable revision/SKU where provided, quantity, units, rider/source and hard constraints. The supplier's compliant bid must provide the designated item; a different product is a separate alternative with a visible 'deviation from exact' classification requiring organiser-authorised approval/waiver, not a silently 'equal' match. Availability/substitution requests do not alter the rider requirement.
* Functional: record the requested **job/outcome** (e.g. one handheld speaking microphone), quantity/units, and explicit hard/soft criteria such as form factor, wireless or wired, intended use, connection/interface, required accessories, safety/compliance, compatibility, performance floor, environment, power/frequency and rental/services scope as applicable. No manufacturer/model need be preselected. 'Generic' must not mean missing functional constraints or no technical check.
* The same event can mix both policies line-by-line and can switch policies through versioned, authorised requirement revisions. Previously sent RFQs or signed contracts are not rewritten; D5-11 impacts and supplier re-quote release approval apply.

### Supplier proposal capture and matching

* Supplier may bid the exact requested item, supply a specific brand/model for a functional request, offer multiple candidate products/options or a design-led solution, each explicitly linked to one or more requirement IDs/versions and source quote lines. Preserve supplied manufacturer/model, quantity, accessories, stock/availability claim and original quote price/conditions separately from organiser's requirement. A proposal can be more detailed than the buyer specification without turning the proposal into the master requirement.
* Group bids by **shared requirement/outcome** in the comparison view. Supplier A offering a Shure microphone, supplier B a RODE microphone and supplier C another named model can be displayed as competing candidates for 'one handheld microphone for speaking', provided they satisfy the hard criteria. Do not mark product identities as literally identical or universally interchangeable: equivalence is **contextual to this requirement**, not a global product master-data assertion.
* Explicit candidate match states: exact match; functionally meets hard criteria (verified); likely fit / proposed alternative (pending review); partially meets; incompatible / material deviation; unable to assess because evidence is missing. Include the reason, criterion/evidence, reviewer or agent provenance, and required accessories/service scope. Never auto-approve uncertain spec claims or rider exceptions, and never treat supplier marketing names as proof of technical suitability. Agents may suggest classification with evidence and confidence but follow authorised human review for ambiguity.
* A supplier may quote an alternative that is better, cheaper, or different, with its rationale and effects on scope, compatibility and price. The organiser may accept the alternative as a **bid choice** without automatically rewriting the original requirement; there is a separate explicit action to adopt it into a new plan/spec revision, with F1 change-impact review and supplier notification gate where applicable.
* Keep price, quantities, taxes, bundle/kit composition, included labour/services and omissions visible alongside equivalence. Comparable in function does not mean commercially equivalent. Do not fabricate component prices for indivisible bundles (D5-7/D5-12). Display competing candidate rows in the same functional grouping, with clear technical differences and quote provenance. Continue neutral bidirectional price-change markers (D5-9/D5-10) across rounds.

### Acceptance checks

* A line requiring an exact microphone model from a performer's rider: only that model/variant meets 'Exact item'; a supplier's different brand/model is visibly a proposed deviation even when acoustically similar. * A line for '1 handheld microphone, spoken-word stage' with approved functional criteria: distinct supplier-branded products can all be matched to that one requirement and shown side-by-side. Missing evidence gives 'pending review', not presumed equivalent.
* A generic lapel requirement and a handheld requirement are **not** grouped as interchangeable unless criteria explicitly permit both. Wireless frequency/licensing, connectors, power, receiver, supplied accessories and use-case requirements can disqualify superficially similar items.

## Decision D5-14 - F6 product specifications and supplier inventory drive equivalence and recommendation (2026-11-13)

### Decision

Following D5-13, equivalence evaluation should preferentially use **structured, evidence-backed technical specifications** from the F6 product/reference catalogue, not solely text similarity or a model's unsourced opinion. When a supplier creates/revises a quote, it can select a known product/kit linked to its inventory or import a new product/datasheet through the controlled F6 product-intake workflow; F5 retains the supplier's immutable original quote/line evidence and links to a specific specification version.

Keep four questions separate in quote comparison: (1) is this the exact required model or a contextually acceptable functional alternative; (2) what technical evidence and gaps support that conclusion; (3) does the supplier's inventory/kit include the required **quantity and components**; (4) is that quantity potentially available for this event window and has the supplier confirmed it? 'Equivalent' never means 'same SKU', 'owned', 'available' or 'contracted'.

Show source-labelled fields (maker, model, variant, type, intended use, specifications, dimensions/weight, electrical/RF/interfaces and accessories where relevant), missing/contradictory values and a comparison of hard vs preferred criteria. Match statuses from D5-13 remain distinct: exact, verified fit, proposed/pending review, partial, incompatible, unknown. An agent may suggest a fit with evidence; missing required data cannot be silently inferred. A supplier manufacturer's marketing text is supporting context, not technical certification. Exact rider models remain exact absent explicit authorised exception.

Supplier stock and availability are tenant-scoped F6 data; the **internal matching process is default-on with explicit supplier opt-out** under <issue id="b22fbb0d-100b-47a3-8c32-eeacadc4c9fe" label="EVE-66" href="https://linear.app/resnovas/issue/EVE-66/f5-directory-and-marketplace-vendor-venue-and-professional-listings" /> D5-16, while all buyer-facing stock/availability evidence remains separately publication-controlled, freshness-labelled and never a wholesale sharing of stock records. Stale or non-shared inventory becomes 'needs confirmation', not 'available' or 'unavailable'. Do not commit, hold, award or send a re-quote merely because the automated check changes; D5-11 manual supplier contact and D5-7 contractual controls remain.
