## Goal

Define how Eventiva's directory and marketplace connect independently isolated tenant databases without broad database access, false global ACID guarantees, or duplication of already settled F5, purchasing, sales and payment decisions.

## Selected architecture and policy details to validate

1. **Publication gateway.** A supplier action emits a versioned, allowlisted public listing projection to a shared directory/search service through an outbox. The projection records source tenant, listing/version, publication consent, provenance and expiry. Unpublish, suspension, consent withdrawal and erasure produce higher-priority tombstones and invalidate caches. The index never reads a supplier database directly.
2. **Marketplace interaction service.** Enquiries, participant grants, quote snapshots, comparison mappings, award plans and workflow correlations live in a controlled marketplace service containing only information both parties are authorised to exchange. Access requires a participant grant scoped to the exact thread/quote/contract and tenant role. A grant never exposes either tenant database.
3. **Tenant-owned canonical records.** Buyer requirements, internal budgets and purchasing records stay in the buyer database. Supplier inventory, availability, private prices, sales records and invoices stay in the supplier database. The marketplace stores immutable exchanged snapshots and identifiers, not writable mirrors of either private system.
4. **Durable orchestration.** Cross-tenant workflows use an idempotent saga/process manager plus per-database transactional outboxes/inboxes. Each step has a stable command id, expected version, retry policy, compensation or manual-recovery state and immutable evidence. Eventiva does not claim one ACID transaction across tenant databases, marketplace storage and payment providers.
5. **Freshness and consent.** Availability, stock, quote validity and private pricing are independently asserted by the owning tenant with as-of time, expiry and confidence/status. Comparison distinguishes verified, stale and unknown. Private prices enter the exchange only through supplier consent or a sent quote.
6. **Payments and ledgers.** The provider-connected supplier account owns the invoice/payment. Signed callbacks are idempotently reconciled into supplier and buyer ledgers plus the marketplace correlation record. Eventiva holds no customer funds and stores no payment credentials.
7. **Mixed awards.** One award plan may select comparable lines from several suppliers, but acceptance creates one immutable contract/quote snapshot, commitment, payment schedule and invoice-reconciliation stream per supplier. Partial selection from an indivisible package requires a supplier-confirmed re-quote. Invoice comparison stays line/requirement aware and never invents component pricing.
8. **Privacy and residency.** Define controller/processor roles, legal basis, participant visibility, retention, export/erasure, moderation access and regional placement separately for public projections, private exchange records and tenant-owned records.

## Alternatives

1. **Shared marketplace database as canonical for all F5 records.** Simpler querying and workflows, but weakens tenant ownership and increases concentration, residency and access risk.
2. **Peer-to-peer tenant service calls only.** Keeps ownership strongest, but makes search, offline delivery, audit, retries and multi-supplier comparison fragile.
3. **Replicate full supplier records into the platform.** Easiest analytics, rejected as the default because it over-collects private inventory, pricing, finance and PII.

## Open decisions for Jonathan

1. The marketplace holds only participant-scoped exchanged snapshots and workflow state; operational canonical records remain tenant-owned. Specify permitted fields and whether participant-visible messages fit that exchange boundary before implementing messages.
2. Participant grants are centrally governed and revocable. Specify and test revocation/retention propagation to offline devices and caches without treating stale cached grants as fresh authorization.
3. How fresh must stock/availability be before quote send, award, contract acceptance and payment? Which steps require a synchronous supplier recheck?
4. What compensation/manual-recovery rules apply when one tenant commits and another tenant or provider step fails?
5. Which data classes may be regionally replicated, and which must remain in the owning tenant's residency boundary?
6. Does invoice reconciliation compare provider invoice lines, supplier accounting lines, or an immutable Eventiva contract schedule when they differ?
7. Which platform staff roles may access private exchange records for moderation, disputes and support, with what audit/approval?

## Required validation for delivery

* Negative authorization matrix across organiser, each supplier, platform moderator, support and agents.
* Publish/unpublish/tombstone tests prove private fields never reach the index and revoked content leaves caches.
* Saga fault injection at every boundary proves no duplicate contract, invoice, commission, stock hold or payment and exposes recoverable partial states honestly.
* Mixed award test with three suppliers preserves separate contracts and invoices while retaining requirement-level comparison/redlines.
* Stale availability, expired quote, revoked participant and delayed/replayed provider callback tests.
* GDPR retention/export/erasure and residency review with named owners.
